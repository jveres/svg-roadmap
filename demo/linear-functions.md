# Linear Functions — A ++_Straight_++ Path Through Algebra :triangular_ruler:

:bulb: Every straight line tells a story of steady change. Build the [core]
ideas first, log some [practice] with real tools, and grab a [challenge]
when you feel unstoppable.

* :one: Get Ready
*:beginner: Lines live on the coordinate plane. Warm up with skills you already ==_half-know_== — where points live, and how one number changes with another.*
  * [The coordinate plane](https://www.mathsisfun.com/data/cartesian-coordinates.html) [core]
    > Two number lines cross, and suddenly every point has an address: (x, y). Learn to read
    > coordinates and name the quadrants — everything else on this map happens here.
    * [Plotting points](https://www.desmos.com/calculator) [practice]
      > Open Desmos and type points like (2, 3). Guess where each one lands before it appears —
      > ten minutes of this makes graphs feel like home turf.
  * [Ratios & rates](https://www.mathsisfun.com/numbers/ratio.html) [core]
    > "3 km every 20 minutes" is a rate: a change in one thing per change in another. Slope is
    > exactly this idea wearing algebra clothes, so make friends with it now.
  * [Solving simple equations](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/equations-solving.html) [core]
    > Keep the equation balanced — whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Every line
    > question that asks "find x" comes back to this move.
  * [What is a function?](https://www.mathsisfun.com/sets/function.html) [core]
    > A function is a machine: every input gets exactly one output. Linear functions are the
    > machines whose output changes by the **same amount** at every step.

* :two: Meet the Line
*:beginner: One line, many costumes. Slope and intercept are the line's ==_DNA_== — every form below is just a different way of writing the same two facts.*
  * [Slope](https://www.mathsisfun.com/gradient.html) [core]
    > Rise over run: how far up (or down) the line moves each time x steps right by one.
    > Positive climbs, negative falls, zero is flat — and steeper means bigger.
    * Slope from two points [core]
      > Pick any two points on the line and divide: (change in y) ÷ (change in x). Any pair
      > gives the same answer — that constancy is what makes the line *linear*.
  * The y-intercept [core]
    > Where the line crosses the y-axis — the starting value before x does anything. In a
    > story problem it is the "you begin with…" number.
  * [Slope-intercept form](https://www.mathsisfun.com/equation_of_line.html) [core]
    > y = mx + b, the friendliest form: m is the slope, b is the y-intercept. Read a line's
    > whole personality from two letters.
    * [Point-slope form](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/line-equation-point-slope.html) [challenge]
      > Know one point and the slope? y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) writes the line without hunting for
      > b first. Rearrange it and slope-intercept falls out.
    * [Standard form](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/linear-equations.html) [challenge]
      > Ax + By = C looks stiff, but it handles vertical lines the other forms cannot, and
      > intercepts pop out by setting x or y to zero.
  * [Graphing lines](https://www.geogebra.org/graphing) [practice]
    > In GeoGebra, graph y = mx + b with sliders for m and b. Drag them and watch the line
    > tilt and glide — after this, no equation will surprise you.
  * Horizontal & vertical lines [core]
    > y = 3 never climbs; x = 2 never runs. One has slope zero, the other has no slope at
    > all — knowing which is which is a classic test question.

* :three: Put It to Work
*:beginner: Lines are everywhere: phone plans, savings, speed. This is where the algebra starts ==_paying rent_==.*
  * [Word problems](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/word-questions-solving.html) [core]
    > Translate the story into y = mx + b: the starting amount is b, the per-something change
    > is m. Once you spot those two numbers, the problem solves itself.
  * [Parallel & perpendicular](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/line-parallel-perpendicular.html) [core]
    > Parallel lines share a slope and never meet; perpendicular slopes multiply to −1. Two
    > small facts, endless geometry questions.
  * [Systems of equations](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/systems-linear-equations.html) [challenge]
    > Two lines, one question: where do they meet? Solve by graphing, substitution, or
    > elimination — the crossing point is the answer to both equations at once.
  * [Linear inequalities](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/graphing-linear-inequalities.html) [challenge]
    > Swap = for < and the answer becomes a whole shaded region. Dashed or solid boundary
    > line? That single detail carries the meaning.
  * [Scatter plots](https://www.mathsisfun.com/data/scatter-xy-plots.html) [practice]
    > Real data never sits perfectly on a line — plot height vs. shoe size and see the cloud.
    > The question becomes: what line comes *closest*?
    * [Line of best fit](https://www.mathsisfun.com/data/least-squares-regression.html) [challenge]
      > One line through the cloud, chosen so the misses are as small as possible. This is
      > your first step into statistics — and it is just y = mx + b again.
  * [Level up on Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/algebra) [practice]
    > The full Algebra 1 course with videos, instant-feedback exercises, and mastery
    > tracking. Ten focused minutes a day beats a panicked night before the exam.

## You've got this :rocket:

Slope is a rate, the intercept is a start, and every form is the same line in
a different costume. Practice a little every day and the straight path stays
straight.
